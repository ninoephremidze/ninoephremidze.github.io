Lensing visualizations

A galaxy behind a galaxy cluster

A galaxy cluster bends the light of a background galaxy into several images. Drag the galaxy in the source plane and watch its images form, merge and split. Click anywhere in the image plane to zoom in there.

Then add dark matter: a single subhalo, which you can drag across the arc, or the granular density fluctuations of wave-like dark matter, which perturb the whole arc at once.

Residual power spectrum

What you are looking at

The cluster is a cored isothermal ellipsoid at z = 0.4 with an Einstein radius of about 18″; the galaxy, cut from a JWST deep field, sits at z = 1 and is 0.75″ (about 6 kpc) across. Each pixel of the image plane is traced back to the source plane through the lens equation β = θ − α(θ).

A real lens model would re-fit the smooth cluster and the source, absorbing any shift or stretch that is uniform across the arc. To show only what such a fit could not absorb, the constant and linear parts of the perturbing deflection (weighted by the arc's brightness) are removed before computing the change in the image.

The wave-like dark matter field is a Gaussian random field whose correlation length is the de Broglie wavelength. Its amplitude here is a free, illustrative parameter, larger than the physical prediction so that the effect is easy to see. The power spectrum of the change in the image is the statistic used in Ephremidze, Gilman & Dvorkin (2026).
